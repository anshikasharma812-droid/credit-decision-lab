# Credit Decision Lab

### From model scores to portfolio strategy

An interactive credit risk strategy case study exploring how predicted probability of default becomes an actual lending decision.

**[→ View the Live Interactive Demo](https://anshikasharma812-droid.github.io/credit-decision-lab/)**

---

## The Business Question

A credit model can estimate the probability that a borrower will default.

But the model does not answer the most important business question:

> **At what level of predicted risk should we change the credit decision?**

A lower risk cutoff can capture more potential defaults, but may also reject more customers who would have performed well.

A higher cutoff can preserve approvals and growth, but exposes the portfolio to additional credit losses.

That makes threshold selection more than a modeling problem. It is a **risk strategy and portfolio economics decision**.

I built Credit Decision Lab to make that trade-off interactive.

---

## What I Built

Credit Decision Lab is an interactive browser-based credit decisioning environment built using **30,000 public credit records** from the UCI Machine Learning Repository.

The project takes a simple credit model and builds a decision-strategy layer around it.

Users can:

* Adjust the probability-of-default (PD) cutoff
* Observe changes in default capture and approval rates
* Examine precision and false-positive trade-offs
* Introduce EAD, LGD and performing-account economics
* Identify an illustrative economically preferred policy cutoff
* Compare predicted PD with observed defaults to examine calibration
* Analyze risk across portfolio segments
* Inspect model drivers
* Test hypothetical applicant profiles
* Translate the analysis into an executive risk-strategy recommendation

The objective is not simply to build the most predictive model.

It is to demonstrate how model output can be translated into **credit policy, portfolio strategy and business decisions**.

---

## Decision Framework

The project separates credit decisioning into several layers:

**1. Risk estimation**

Estimate a borrower's probability of default.

**2. Risk ranking**

Determine whether the model effectively separates higher-risk from lower-risk accounts.

**3. Calibration**

Test whether predicted probabilities resemble actual observed default rates.

**4. Policy threshold**

Determine where predicted risk becomes high enough to change the credit decision.

**5. Portfolio economics**

Evaluate the cost of accepting additional default risk against the value generated by performing accounts.

**6. Portfolio monitoring**

Examine whether risk is concentrated within particular borrower or behavioral segments.

**7. Strategy recommendation**

Translate quantitative analysis into an operating recommendation while identifying what would need to be validated before production deployment.

This distinction is important because **model performance and credit strategy are related, but they are not the same thing**.

## Interactive Threshold Lab

The central experiment allows users to change the PD cutoff and immediately observe the impact on:

* **Default Capture / Recall** — how many observed defaults the strategy identifies
* **Precision** — how concentrated actual defaults are among flagged accounts
* **Approval / Pass-Through Rate** — how much of the portfolio remains eligible
* **False-Positive Rate** — how many non-defaulting accounts would be flagged

Moving the cutoff demonstrates a fundamental credit strategy trade-off:

**Risk protection ↔ Customer approval / growth**

There is no universally correct threshold. The appropriate operating point depends on the economics and risk appetite of the lending product.

## Expected Loss & Policy Economics

V2 introduces an illustrative economic decision layer.

Users can adjust:

* **EAD — Exposure at Default**
* **LGD — Loss Given Default**
* **Contribution from a performing account**

The lab then evaluates candidate PD thresholds using a simplified economic framework.

Conceptually:

**Expected Loss = PD × LGD × EAD**

The decision layer also considers the value generated by accounts that are approved and do not default.

This demonstrates why the threshold with the highest statistical performance is not necessarily the threshold that produces the best business outcome.

> **The optimal model cutoff and the optimal business policy cutoff may be different.**

The economics in this project are deliberately simplified and illustrative. A production lending strategy would additionally consider factors such as utilization, funding costs, acquisition costs, pricing, recovery behavior, lifetime value and capital requirements.

## Model Calibration

A model can rank customers correctly while still producing probabilities that are poorly calibrated.

For example, a group of customers assigned approximately 20% PD should, over an appropriate observation period, default at roughly that rate if the model is well calibrated.

The lab groups holdout observations into predicted-risk bands and compares:

**Average predicted PD vs. observed default rate**

This demonstrates the distinction between:

* **Discrimination:** Can the model rank risk correctly?
* **Calibration:** Can its predicted probabilities be interpreted as realistic probabilities?

That distinction becomes especially important when model outputs are used for underwriting, pricing, exposure management or expected-loss estimation.

## Portfolio Segmentation

Portfolio-level averages can hide important concentrations of risk.

The lab therefore examines default behavior across segments such as:

* Repayment status
* Credit-limit bands

This introduces a broader portfolio-management question:

> Is the risk increasing across the entire book, or is deterioration concentrated within a specific segment?

In a production environment, this analysis could be extended to include vintages, customer cohorts, utilization bands, geography, acquisition channel, industry, tenure and other relevant dimensions.

## Model Drivers

The project includes a standardized logistic-regression driver view.

Modeled variables include signals related to:

* Credit limit
* Age
* Recent repayment status
* Historical repayment behavior
* Bill amounts
* Recent payment amounts

The goal is not to claim causal relationships.

Instead, the driver view helps examine which variables are contributing most strongly to the model's risk ranking.

This distinction matters:

> **Predictive signal is not the same as causality.**

A variable can help predict default without explaining why default occurs.

## Applicant Sandbox

The interactive sandbox allows users to modify a hypothetical account profile, including characteristics such as:

* Credit limit
* Age
* Repayment status
* Bill amount
* Payment amount

The model generates an estimated PD and compares it with the selected policy threshold.

This makes the relationship between:

**Applicant characteristics → Model score → Policy threshold → Credit decision**

directly observable.

## Risk Strategy Recommendation

The final layer converts the quantitative analysis into an illustrative executive recommendation.

Rather than simply returning a model metric, the project asks:

* What cutoff appears economically attractive?
* What happens to approval rates?
* How much default risk is captured?
* How sensitive is the decision to the economic assumptions?
* What additional validation would be required before deployment?

A production implementation would require additional work including:

* Segment-level validation
* Fairness and compliance review
* Calibration validation
* Sensitivity analysis
* Stability testing
* Vintage monitoring
* Model and policy drift monitoring
* Controlled rollout
* Champion/challenger testing

The purpose is to demonstrate that **credit strategy does not end when the model is built**.

---

## Model Development

For this portfolio case study, the site trains a lightweight **logistic regression model directly in the browser** using JavaScript.

The implementation uses a deterministic development/holdout structure and a selected set of credit and repayment variables.

Logistic regression was intentionally chosen because the objective of this project is not to maximize algorithmic complexity.

It provides an interpretable foundation for demonstrating the more important strategic layer:

**How do we convert predicted risk into an operating credit policy?**

A production environment would require substantially more rigorous model development, validation, governance and monitoring.

## Key Takeaway

The central idea behind Credit Decision Lab is simple:

> **A better predictive model does not automatically create a better credit decision.**

Models estimate risk.

Risk strategy determines what to do with that estimate.

The strongest credit decisioning systems connect **model performance, portfolio economics, customer impact and risk appetite** into a coherent operating policy.
